# ACRM — Human–AI Research Profile

## Research context

ACRM (Adaptive Cognitive Regulation Module) is an independent research and engineering effort focused on observable, evaluable, and governable behavior in LLM-based systems.

The project emerged through an iterative human–AI research process involving long-horizon interaction, hypothesis formation, architectural exploration, prototyping, implementation, testing, and refinement.

## Research trajectory

The development path can be summarized as:

Observation → Research Question → Hypothesis → Architecture → Prototype → Contract → Implementation → Testing → Evaluation

The purpose of preserving this trajectory is not to claim validation beyond available evidence, but to provide transparency about how the architecture evolved.

## Core research direction

ACRM investigates questions around:

- behavioral stability during long-running LLM interactions;
- state evolution and trajectory consistency;
- observability and governance boundaries;
- separation between observed behavior and interpretation;
- engineering methods for making evolving AI systems more reviewable.

## Human–AI collaboration model

Language models were used as analytical collaborators during exploration, critique, comparison, and refinement phases. The human researcher remained responsible for integration, architectural decisions, implementation choices, and evidence evaluation.

## Evidence boundary

Research observations, conceptual models, prototypes, software contracts, implementations, tests, and empirical validation represent different evidence levels and should not be conflated.

ACRM follows an evidence-first principle: concepts become engineering components only after explicit responsibilities, contracts, implementations, tests, and appropriate evaluation methods exist.
